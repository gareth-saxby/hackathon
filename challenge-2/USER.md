# User Profile — Who This Tool Is For

## Primary User: Government Caseworker

The primary user of this tool is a **government caseworker**, specialising in housing and employment. They work on the frontline of public services — processing claims, assessing eligibility, and advising citizens on their rights and entitlements. Their caseload spans housing benefit, council tax reduction, employment rights, and small business support, and on any given day they may be asked about any of these areas with little warning.

This tool exists because the knowledge caseworkers need is already published — it just is not accessible under the time pressure of real work. The tool does not replace the caseworker's expertise or judgement. It removes the friction between knowing that guidance exists and being able to use it at the moment it is needed.

---

## The caseworker's day

A government caseworker specialising in housing and employment does not work from memory alone. Policy is complex, it changes, and the guidance that underpins decisions is spread across dozens of documents — some current, some outdated, some contradicting each other in ways nobody has formally resolved.

The caseworker knows the guidance exists. The problem is retrieving the right part of it quickly enough to be useful. When a citizen is on the phone or sitting across a desk, there is no time to search a shared drive, open three documents with similar names, and read until the relevant paragraph appears.

**The result, without this tool:**
- Answers given based on whichever document was found first, not the most accurate
- Uncertainty about whether the version retrieved is still current
- Time spent on document retrieval that should be spent on the citizen
- Decisions that could be challenged because the wrong version of guidance was applied

**The result, with this tool:**
- A question typed in plain English returns the specific passage that answers it
- Every result shows the source document, section, publication date, and a confidence level
- Quality flags surface stale, superseded, or contradicted content before it is relied upon
- The caseworker stays in control of the decision — the tool handles the retrieval

---

## The problem this solves

The guidance caseworkers need already exists. It is published. It is official. But it is locked inside PDFs and Word documents on shared drives, inconsistently named, with no reliable way to know which version is current or whether two documents on the same topic agree with each other.

Finding the right answer today means:
- Searching a shared drive and finding three documents with similar names
- Opening a 60-page PDF and using Ctrl+F, hoping the search term matches the document's language
- Not being sure whether the version retrieved is still current
- Giving an answer based on the document found fastest, not the one that is most accurate

This tool solves that specific problem. A caseworker types a question in plain English. The tool searches across all official guidance documents, finds the most relevant passage, and returns it with the source, section, date, and a flag if anything about that content warrants caution. The guidance was always there. Now it is findable.

---

## Why this user, and why now

Government caseworkers are the point at which policy meets people. When they have the right information, citizens get accurate answers, decisions are defensible, and avoidable callbacks and corrections are reduced. When they do not, the costs fall on citizens who receive wrong advice, on teams who handle follow-up contacts, and on caseworkers themselves who carry the risk of applying guidance incorrectly.

The caseworker is also the user most constrained by time. A developer can take an hour to find an answer. A policy analyst can read the full document. A caseworker has a citizen waiting. The tool is designed specifically for that constraint.

## What the tool does and does not do for this user

The tool is a retrieval aid, not a decision-maker. It is designed to handle the part of the caseworker's job that should not require human effort — finding the right passage in the right document — so the caseworker can focus on the part that does: applying judgement, talking to citizens, and making accurate decisions.

**It does:**
- Return the specific passage from official guidance that is most relevant to a plain-English query
- Show the source document, section, and publication date alongside every result
- Flag content that may be stale, superseded, or contradicted by another document
- Surface cross-domain results — for example, a query about self-employment and housing benefit returns results from both the housing and employment document sets

**It does not:**
- Make eligibility decisions or recommendations
- Replace the caseworker's training, professional judgement, or escalation responsibilities
- Access live case management systems, benefit accounts, or citizen records
- Interpret law — it surfaces published guidance, not legal advice

## How to use the tool

1. **Type your question** in plain English — the way a citizen would ask it, or the way you would describe it to a colleague
2. **Review the results** — each result shows:
   - The matching passage from the guidance
   - The document it came from (with ID and title)
   - The section heading
   - The publication date
   - A relevance score and confidence level
3. **Check the quality flags** — if a result is marked as **STALE**, **SUPERSEDED**, or **CONTRADICTION**, treat it with caution and check the alternative document shown
4. **Use the source reference** — every result tells you exactly which document and section the answer came from, so you can cite it accurately in correspondence or decisions

---

## What the tool does not do

- It does not make decisions for you — it finds the guidance, you apply the judgement
- It does not replace your training or your manager — if a case is complex or borderline, escalate as normal
- It does not access live systems (Universal Credit, council tax accounts, etc.) — it searches guidance documents only
- It is not a legal advice service — it surfaces official government guidance, not legal interpretation

## Understanding the relevance score

Every result includes a **relevance score** and a **confidence level** (High / Medium / Low). These tell the caseworker how closely the returned passage matches the query — not how correct the guidance itself is.

| Score | What it means for the caseworker |
|-------|----------------------------------|
| **High confidence** | The passage closely matches the query. The document directly addresses the topic searched. Use it with normal professional judgement. |
| **Medium confidence** | The passage is related but may not be a precise match. The document covers the topic area but the specific answer may require reading more context. |
| **Low confidence** | The passage is the closest match found, but the tool is not certain it directly answers the question. Read the full section before relying on it, or try a different search term. |

A high relevance score does not mean the guidance is current or free of issues — always check the quality flags alongside it. A low score does not mean the result is wrong — it means the query language and document language may not overlap closely, and rephrasing the search may return a better match.

## Quality flags — what they mean

| Flag | What it means | What to do |
|------|--------------|------------|
| 🟡 **STALE** | The document's publication date is more than 12 months ago | Check whether a newer version exists before relying on this |
| 🔴 **SUPERSEDED** | Another document has replaced this one | Use the newer document shown in the results |
| 🟠 **CONTRADICTION** | Two documents cover the same topic but say different things | Note both sources and escalate if the discrepancy affects your decision |
| ✅ **Current** | No issues detected | Proceed with normal professional judgement |
